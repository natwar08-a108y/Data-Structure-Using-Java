Congruence Relation: Let m be a positive integer.
Two integers a and b are said to be congruent modulo m if their
difference (a - b) is perfectly divisible by m.

a ≡ b (mod m) ⇔ m | (a − b)
a & b leave the exact same remainder when divided by m.

Residue Classes:
Modulo m divides the infinite universe of integers into exactly
m distinct sets called complete residue classes.

For a modulus m, the standard residue classes are:
[0], [1], [2], ..., [m - 1]
Where:
[r] = {..., r - 2m, r - m, r, r + m, r + 2m, ...}

Modular Multiplicative Inverse:
The modular inverse of an integer a modulo m is an integer x such that:
ax ≡ 1 (mod m)

The Golden Rule:
The modular inverse x exists if and only if:
gcd(a, m) = 1
i.e., a and m are coprime.

How to solve:
We find the modular inverse using the Extended Euclidean
Algorithm.

Since:
gcd(a, m) = 1

Bézout's identity states:
ax + my = 1

Taking modulo m on both sides drops the my term, giving:
ax ≡ 1 (mod m)

Thus, the coefficient x is our inverse!
